# Offhour — five ways to make time

**Kind:** Single-product direct-to-consumer ecommerce brand.

**Name:** Offhour.

**Positioning:** A mechanical desk timer that gives your next hour a clear boundary, without another screen.

**Customer:** People who work or create at a desk and reach for their phone more often than they want to: independent designers, writers, remote workers, students, and thoughtful gift buyers.

**Pricing:** $48 once, with US shipping included. Applicable tax would be shown at a future live checkout. Three proposed anodized aluminum finishes: warm sand, burnt orange, and slate blue. Planned first shipment November 2026, cancellation before shipment, and 30-day returns after delivery. A two-person team could develop the enclosure around an existing mechanical movement and launch a small manufactured batch within a year.

**Product:** A wind-up visual countdown timer, adjustable from 1–60 minutes, with an end bell and a soft mechanical tick. Target dimensions: 80 mm diameter × 45 mm depth. No batteries, apps, subscriptions, or accounts. These are concept specifications, not certified production claims.

## 01 — The everyday ritual

`/themes/1` uses warm paper, olive, and sand; large literary serif headlines; an asymmetric split hero; and a custom SVG product still life on a book under simulated window light. The voice is personal and unhurried. Benefits unfold as a three-part ritual before the hardware details and FAQs. This direction is aimed at design-conscious remote workers and gift buyers who want a useful object that belongs in their home. Its credibility comes from transparent first-edition language and concrete specifications, rather than invented endorsements.

## 02 — The attention rebellion

`/themes/2` is a graphic campaign in acid yellow, off-white, and black. Oversized heavy sans-serif typography, outlined lettering, hard rules, square buttons, and a semicircular product stage make it feel like a poster that happens to sell a timer. The copy is direct: reclaim your attention, start a task, and actually stop. A manifesto, benefit columns, a four-part specification strip, and a blunt FAQ replace the gentler storytelling of the first direction. It targets younger creatives and people already frustrated with their screen habits. The graphite product illustration is a design study; the available preorder-preview finishes remain sand, orange, and blue.

## 03 — The quiet object

`/themes/3` treats Offhour as a gallery object. Cool slate blue, restrained system sans-serif labels, delicate italic serif headlines, a centered composition, and an original product render on an architectural pedestal establish a contemplative pace. A philosophical opening is followed by an asymmetric object study, a clearly attributed fictional founder statement, and a compact FAQ. The copy makes room for reading, thinking, and rest alongside work. It is aimed at minimalists and considered-design buyers who respond to restraint and materiality. The founder quotation belongs to this invented brand; it is not a customer testimonial.

## 04 — The good-time club

`/themes/4` is cheerful retro retail: butter yellow, terracotta, muted green, chunky italic serif type, an arched grid backdrop, smiling SVG flowers, stickers, and hand-drawn underlines. The layout alternates an illustrated hero, a full-width slogan ribbon, rounded benefit cards, numbered instructions, and a candid small-team introduction. The tone gives visitors permission to make time for hobbies and breaks, without turning every hour into a productivity exercise. It targets students, hobbyists, and gift shoppers who want personality and an accessible price.

## 05 — The focus instrument

`/themes/5` uses graphite, sage, monospace annotations, measured spacing, a drafting grid, product callouts, and a specification table. Its asymmetrical hero presents the timer as a physical tool for deep work. A three-stage workflow leads into detailed hardware specifications, an explicit prelaunch status panel, field notes, and a substantial final CTA. It is aimed at developers, makers, and people who prefer to understand exactly how a tool fits into their working system. The dark finish is explicitly identified as a design study, while the preview offers the three planned finishes.

## Recommendation

Ship **01 — The everyday ritual**. It communicates the practical benefit immediately while making a modest $48 object feel desirable and at home on a real desk. The restrained palette and editorial product imagery support both self-purchase and gifting, giving it a broader audience than the campaign-like second direction or technical fifth direction. The concept still needs real manufacturing samples, photography, and operational shipping and return policies before commercial launch.

## Implementation and conversion

All routes include an above-the-fold primary CTA, repeated at the end, benefits before detailed features, visible pricing, honest prelaunch credibility, and expandable FAQs. `/` is a visual index linking to all five routes. All visuals use inline SVG and CSS, and all fonts use local system stacks; no external images, fonts, or UI libraries are requested.

The primary CTA opens an accessible native dialog with finish selection, price, shipping context, and an explicit concept-store explanation. “Save my selection” stores only the selected finish and price in this browser’s local storage. It collects no email or payment information, and clearly states that saving is not an order or reservation. The dialog supports Escape, focus containment, backdrop dismissal, and a visible close button. No checkout backend or real orders are represented as working.
